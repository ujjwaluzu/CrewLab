# CrewLab keyword research

**Prepared:** 18 October 2026  
**Market assumption:** India first, English language, based on the site's `en_IN` metadata and India geo settings.  
**Product context:** CrewLab connects project owners and builders around startup and side projects. It supports project discovery, join requests, crew coordination, and optional read-only GitHub context. It is not positioned as a paid hiring or freelance marketplace.

## Executive recommendation

Lead with **find a cofounder / technical cofounder in India** for founders and **join startup or side projects** for builders. These are high intent, product aligned searches and match the way current India focused platforms describe their offers. Give each intent its own landing page. Keep “hire developers” out of the core target set: current results for that wording skew toward recruiting, staffing, and freelance marketplaces, which is a different offer.

Treat priorities below as qualitative. No keyword volume, difficulty, conversion, or first party Search Console data was available in this research, so numeric estimates would be misleading. Search result observations are directional and vary by location and time.

## Page positioning and content changes

### Homepage `/`

- **Primary theme:** find startup collaborators and build projects together.
- **Suggested title:** `Find Cofounders & Startup Teammates in India | CrewLab`
- **Suggested H1 direction:** “Find your cofounder and build your startup with a crew.” Keep the current brand voice in the supporting copy.
- Name both sides of the marketplace early: founders with an idea and builders looking for a project.
- Add clear links to `/find-cofounder`, `/explore`, and `/side-projects` near the first relevant copy.

### `/find-cofounder`

- **Primary theme:** find a cofounder or technical cofounder for a startup.
- **Suggested title:** `Find a Cofounder in India | CrewLab`
- Mention India explicitly in visible copy if India is the deliberate target market, not only in hidden geo metadata.
- Explain how project context, skills, contribution, and availability help people evaluate fit. Avoid claims about automated or compatibility matching.
- Add a useful FAQ: where to find a cofounder in India, how to find a technical cofounder, and what to discuss before joining a startup.

### `/explore`

- **Primary theme:** startup projects looking for collaborators.
- **Suggested title:** `Explore Startup Projects to Join | CrewLab`
- Make the page indexable value distinct from a directory of sample cards. If listings are illustrative, label them clearly and add substantial guidance about project stages, skills, and joining.
- As real public projects become available, give each a crawlable page with a descriptive title, summary, requested skills, and owner-approved public details. Avoid indexing thin or private listings.

### `/find-developers`

- **Primary theme:** find developers who want to build with a startup team.
- Keep page copy explicit about joining or collaborating. Do not imply a shortlist of vetted hires, staffing service, paid freelance work, or employment placement unless CrewLab adds those services.
- Link to cofounder and project discovery pages when a searcher may actually be looking for a technical partner or a project to join.

### `/startup-teams`, `/side-projects`, `/student-projects`

- Keep these separate because they serve distinct audiences and jobs to be done.
- Use one main query theme per page and avoid repeating the same generic “find your crew” copy across all pages.
- Add concrete examples and FAQs specific to each audience, using language drawn from actual user questions.

## Supporting content opportunities

Prioritize helpful pages that answer a real next-step question and link to the relevant landing page:

1. **How to find a technical cofounder in India** — compare routes, outline fit and commitment questions, and explain how to assess a project together.
2. **What to discuss before joining a startup as a cofounder** — roles, time commitment, decision making, ownership, and expectations. Keep legal guidance general and source any jurisdiction specific advice.
3. **Where developers can find startup projects to join** — explain how to evaluate scope, team expectations, and contribution before applying.
4. **How to write a startup project listing that attracts collaborators** — include project stage, problem, requested skills, time expectations, and what applicants should know.
5. **Side projects for developers: how to choose one and contribute well** — practical selection advice, linking to project discovery.

Do not publish thin keyword variations (for example, separate pages for “find a cofounder” and “find a co-founder”) or generic AI generated lists of startup ideas. The useful differentiator is project first collaboration with clear owner reviewed requests and shared project context.

## Search result and competitor observations

- India specific cofounder pages repeatedly use phrases such as “find a co-founder in India,” skills, startup stage, and building a founding team. This supports an India localized cofounder page as the strongest initial SEO bet. Examples: [Foundrr](https://www.foundrr.co/), [ConnectX](https://getconnectx.app/find-a-cofounder-india), [CoFounder Dekho](https://cofounderdekho.in/find-a-cofounder), and [Foundersbase India](https://in.foundersbase.com/).
- “Find developers for startup” search results include hiring guides and recruiting platforms such as [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-to-find-developers-for-startups) and [Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/hire). That wording carries a hiring expectation; CrewLab should qualify its page around collaborators and people joining a project.
- Builder communities describe project discovery around “open projects,” “join a team,” “build real projects,” and proof of work. [The Developer Community](https://www.thedevcommunity.in/) is an example of this adjacent language. CrewLab can distinguish itself by centering the project, owner reviewed join request, and ongoing crew collaboration.

These observations come from a live search results review, not a measured keyword database. Competitor page copy indicates language and market positioning, not their traffic or rankings.

## Measurement and validation plan

1. Verify the canonical domain in Google Search Console and submit the sitemap.
2. After indexing and enough impressions, export Search Console Performance data by **query, page, and country**. Use India as the primary segment, while retaining a global view if CrewLab accepts international users.
3. Compare impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position for the clusters above. Add actual query variants surfaced by GSC; prioritize pages with impressions and positions near page one, and rewrite titles where impressions are strong but CTR is weak.
4. Use Google Ads Keyword Planner or another paid keyword database if monthly volume and difficulty estimates are needed. Record geography, language, match type, date, and source with every number.
5. Track signup conversions by landing page so rankings are evaluated against relevant users, not traffic alone.

Google Search Console provides actual queries, impressions, clicks, CTR, and position for the property; its query table may omit anonymized searches. [Search Console Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en). Google says the meta-keywords tag has no effect on Google Search; page titles, visible copy, helpful content, and internal linking matter more than expanding the metadata keyword list. [Google supported meta tags](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/special-tags), [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).
